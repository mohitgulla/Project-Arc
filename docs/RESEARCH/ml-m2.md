## Dev decision (pre-registered `m2_promote`)

**Result: no, for both families.** Both versions are registered as `candidate`; neither
becomes `shadow`, so per D133 the holdout was **not read** (it is read only when the
owner promotes a shadow to champion). A no is a valid outcome for this card: the
harness, calibration, registry and replay hook ship and are what E27.4–E27.9 build on.

What failed, in order of how much it matters:

1. **Decision value (the metric that matters): `e_r` as the ranker loses to the
   incumbent (`managed_net_ev`) in 3 of 4 family-years on net and in 4 of 4 on max DD.**
   Credit 2023 is the clearest: the incumbent made +$21.9k, `ml_er` lost $9.5k.
   The ranking metrics explain it: **the incumbent key itself out-ranks `e_r`** in
   2022 (per-day IC of `managed_net_ev` 0.157 vs 0.070 credit, 0.189 vs 0.086 debit) and
   is roughly level in 2023 (0.119 vs 0.137 credit, 0.149 vs 0.146 debit). E7.17's
   negative IC of managed EV was on *opened* live trades; over the whole sim menu it
   ranks well, so the bar M2 has to clear is higher than the card assumed. Beyond
   that, the sim takes the top of a 5+5-slot book, where the gate, buying power and
   cluster caps decide most of the outcome, and M2 is trained on the whole menu
   (mostly mediocre candidates), not on the top of it.
2. **The filters are worse.** `ml_er_pos` (skip `e_r ≤ 0`) leaves almost nothing in 2023
   (credit 1 trade, debit 0), because `e_r` is negative for 99.98% of 2023 candidates
   (mean predicted −0.09 vs realised −0.09 / −0.11: the level is right, every candidate
   just has negative expectancy at x = 0.25 slippage). `ml_pstop` (skip `p_stop ≥ τ`)
   helped credit 2022 (−16.2k vs −18.6k, DD −76.5% vs −77.8%: the only use that beat
   the incumbent on both) and hurt the other three.
3. **Probabilistic: the calibrated `p_win` loses to a logistic regression on the same
   features in all four family-years, and debit also loses to managed PoP.**
   - Calibration error is the cause, not ranking: win ECE after the isotonic map is
     0.07–0.12, and the *uncalibrated* booster is better in 2023 (debit raw ECE 0.009,
     log-loss 0.686 vs 0.715 calibrated). The isotonic map is fitted on the year before
     the test year (2021 for test-2022, 2022 for test-2023), and the win base rate moves
     between years (credit 50.2% → 50.7%, but debit p_win means 0.39 in 2022 vs 0.55 in
     2023 against base rates 0.45 / 0.44), so a one-year-back map transfers a regime
     level that the next year does not have.
   - Shipping the inner booster with its own map (protocol rev 2, tried in this card and
     reverted) scored worse in 3 of 4 fold-years, so the problem is the year-to-year
     shift, not a train/calibrate mismatch.
   - The stop classifier is fine: credit stop AUC 0.74, ECE 0.04; it beats logistic in
     2022 and is within 0.03 in 2023.
4. **IC t-stat in 2022 is below 2** for both families (1.47 / 1.77; 2023 is 13–14). 2022 was
   a bear year whose ranking the 2020–21 train window had not seen.

`e_r` vs the mixture cross-check `p_win·E[r|win] + (1−p_win)·E[r|loss]`: the two agree in
level (credit 2023 means −0.090 vs −0.052) but the mixture spreads 5× wider (sd 0.14 vs
0.026), so the Huber regressor is strongly shrunk toward the mean, which is part of
why `ml_er_pos` collapses.

- 3× more rows did **not** help: IC is slightly *higher* at 200/day in every
  family-year, and log-loss is mixed. More near-duplicate candidates from the same
  day add little information (n_eff grows 1.6–2.5×, not 3×). The cap-200 credit model
  even beats logistic on 2022 log-loss (0.689 vs 0.690), the only such cell.
- Peak RSS (~8.1–8.3 GB on the 16 GB host) is set by loading the feature store and
  the ledger partitions, not by the sample size; training fits comfortably.
- Scoring one day's menu takes ~0.03 s (≈ 250–300 candidates).
- The registered versions are the pre-registered cap-600 runs; cap-200 versions are
  study output only (not registered).

## What this means for the next cards

- **E27.4 (shadow logging)** has no shadow model to log; it can log a `candidate` model
  for live-vs-sim calibration if the owner wants that, but nothing should rank by it.
- The strongest lead is the `p_stop` model (AUC 0.74 on credit) as a skip filter or a
  sizing input, and a ranking target trained on what the book actually takes (top of
  menu after the gate) rather than the whole menu: both are ML-<n> study material
  (D133) through `--overlay`, not changes to this card's pre-registered protocol.
- Calibration needs a regime-robust approach (e.g. calibrate on a rolling multi-year
  window, or condition on `ctx_trend`/`ctx_vol`); the per-regime ECE in each model card
  shows the bias is largest in 2022's bear/sideways regimes.
